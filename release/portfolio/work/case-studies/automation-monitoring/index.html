ErrorLens: Make broken automations visible and actionable.

ErrorLens gives teams a clear place to investigate workflow failures. Execution details, severity, suggested next steps and incident ownership sit together, while logging and Slack alert paths bring the failure context into the team’s daily operating rhythm.

1 / 6

What it does

  • Incident review interface with filters, failure detail and assigned ownership.
  • Failure-context views covering classification, severity and suggested next steps.
  • Recovery review controls with incident status and a local resolved state.

Why it matters

Makes failure investigation and responsibility easier to coordinate, with the context needed to decide the next recovery action.

The client workspace

How the system is built

  1. SourcesFailed n8n executions; broader interface categories include Make, Zapier and custom systems.
  2. IngestionRecovered n8n source supports scheduled/webhook collection of recent failures.
  3. NormalizationExecution identity, workflow reference, error category and severity.
  4. StorageSource-grounded Google Sheets logging through its HTTP API; optional designed Postgres incident store.
  5. DecisionsSeven-category source classification, alert routing and human recovery review.
  6. DashboardReact incident overview, filters, ownership and local resolution controls.
  7. Write-backsSheets log entries and Slack alerts; workflow repair remains separately authorized.

The dashboard

14 views: Today, Operations summary, Open incidents, Acknowledged, Resolved, Incident detail, n8n, Zapier, Make, Custom, Alert rules, Routing and on-call, Credentials expiring, Audit log.

The 24-hour error ribbon: hours by platform as a grid, severity shown by fill and symbol, each cell opening the incidents behind it.

State lives in the URL (view, filters, date range), records open in drawers with their history, and every action that changes something is written to an activity log and is safe to repeat.

Data model

  • platforms(id, name, connection_ref)
  • workflows(id, platform_id, external_id, owner_id)
  • executions(id, workflow_id, external_id, failed_at)
  • incidents(id, execution_id, category, severity, state)
  • rules(id, version, matcher, category, suggested_action)
  • assignments(id, incident_id, owner_id, assigned_at)
  • notifications(id, incident_id, destination, state)
  • recovery_checks(id, incident_id, destination_ref, evidence, checked_at).

Rules in the backend

  1. Exclude the monitoring workflow’s own executions, as the recovered source does.
  2. Proposed collector window: poll every five minutes with a ten-minute overlap and deduplicate by platform plus execution ID.
  3. Preserve the original error while applying a versioned classification; unknown patterns remain unclassified.
  4. Proposed alert suppression: one initial alert per incident, with further alerts only for severity escalation or an approved reminder.
  5. A local resolved flag does not prove recovery; require recorded destination evidence for verified closure.

Storage

Preserve Google Sheets as the source logging destination. Recommend Supabase Postgres with tenant RLS for multi-user incident ownership and audit history. Redact secrets and personal payload fields before either destination. Proposed raw-error retention is 14 days; sanitized incidents/audits are retained for 12 months.

Reliability

Collector retries must not recursively generate duplicate incidents. Use unique execution keys, an alert outbox and dead-letter review. Show collection freshness, notification failure and “recovery unverified” separately.

Security and access

Viewers inspect sanitized incidents; operators assign and investigate; authorized administrators approve repair actions. Provider tokens and raw sensitive payloads remain server-side.

Cost

Monitoring requests, n8n execution volume, storage, Sheets quotas and Slack plan requirements may affect cost. Reported pre-build client losses do not establish retained clients or savings.

The failure had a business cost

Ahmad reports that the commissioning company lost approximately three clients in two months when automations broke. That is the reason for the system, not proof that the same amount was subsequently saved. The recovered source shows how failures are collected, classified and put in front of the team.

Collect the failure before asking someone to fix it

The original creator workflow uses a schedule or webhook, fetches failed n8n executions, filters recent failures and excludes its own runs. The local capture shows that actual source topology, imported without credentials and never executed.

Classify, log and alert

The source classifies failures into seven categories with severity and a suggested next step. Log to Sheet is an HTTP Request to the Google Sheets API, followed by the Slack alert path. A native Google Sheets node is not required for that logging step.

Make recovery a human-owned decision

The broader ErrorLens interface adds the review surface: filters, incident detail, an owner and a local resolved state. An alert or a local resolved flag does not prove the affected business record was repaired. The responsible person still checks the destination.
