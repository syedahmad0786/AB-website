Command Center: Run client delivery from a shared operating workspace.

Command Center brings client ownership, lifecycle milestones, service context and report review into a shared agency workspace. Teams can follow the client journey, inspect provider collection results and move the next handoff forward with clear responsibility.

1 / 6

What it does

  • Client and staff ownership linked to lifecycle milestones, services and activity.
  • Provider collection receipts and data-gap visibility alongside client reporting.
  • Versioned report review with role-based access and separately scoped company knowledge.

Why it matters

Gives account teams a common place to understand client status, assign responsibility and review delivery readiness.

The client workspace

Shown as the workspace for Practice Lift, a patient-acquisition agency for chiropractic and wellness clinics.

How the system is built

  1. SourcesGoHighLevel client context, service records, Meta and Google Ads evidence.
  2. IngestionDesigned n8n collection and Deno server-side handlers with collection receipts.
  3. NormalizationConfirmed client/provider mappings, milestone types and staff ownership.
  4. StorageSupabase PostgreSQL for tenant-scoped operating records and report versions.
  5. DecisionsLifecycle readiness, evidence completeness, review and access boundaries.
  6. DashboardClient lifecycle, delivery operations and read-only reporting.
  7. Write-backsSeparately authorized CRM updates and reviewed communications; unavailable in the walkthrough.

The dashboard

14 views: Today, Agency summary, Client list, Client record, Onboarding, Milestone pipeline, Surveys and NPS, Upsells, Email drafts, Calls and notes, Services, Reports, GHL sync, Activity log.

The Day 30/60/90 rail: every clinic on a 120-day axis with its three milestone gates, overdue gates marked, and the draft for the next check-in one click away.

State lives in the URL (view, filters, date range), records open in drawers with their history, and every action that changes something is written to an activity log and is safe to repeat.

Data model

  • clients(id, tenant_id, ghl_id, owner_id, lifecycle_stage)
  • staff(id, tenant_id, role, active)
  • milestones(id, client_id, type, due_at, status)
  • services(id, client_id, service_type, owner_id, status)
  • provider_mappings(id, client_id, platform, external_id, confirmed_at)
  • collection_receipts(id, mapping_id, period, status, collected_at)
  • report_versions(id, client_id, version, review_state, evidence_refs)
  • activity_events(id, client_id, actor_id, action, occurred_at).

Rules in the backend

  1. Generate Day 30/60/90 milestones once per client and lifecycle anchor.
  2. Read provider details only through confirmed client/account mappings.
  3. Missing collection evidence remains missing, never a zero-performance result.
  4. Approval applies to a specific report version; edits invalidate that approval.
  5. Keep privately ingested knowledge separate from reviewed shared material.

Storage

Supabase PostgreSQL is supported by inspected application-source context. Recommend tenant RLS, private object storage and append-only review history. Proposed raw collection retention is 30 days; report/audit retention is 24 months. Mask personal emails and exclude patient-level information unless separately scoped and authorized.

Reliability

The designed backend uses idempotent milestone creation, collection retries and an outbox for approved writes. Show last successful synchronization, partial provider coverage and failed receipts. A collected report and a delivered report require different states.

Security and access

Separate staff operators, report reviewers, administrators and read-only clients. Enforce tenant/client authorization server-side. Keep Supabase service credentials and provider secrets out of the browser.

Cost

Hosting, database/storage, n8n executions, CRM licensing and optional model/report processing may incur charges. No saved hours, subscription replacement or revenue benefit is established.

One client record instead of a status hunt

The operating problem is scattered context: who owns the client, what stage they are at, which provider evidence is available and what needs a review decision. The command-center layer brings those questions together rather than asking a rep to reconstruct them from separate screens.

Show the real operating views

The local interface includes lifecycle, dashboard, clients, milestones, services, email drafts, activity and read-only reporting views. Provider setup, sends and actions that require a real connection remain unavailable in this private walkthrough.

Keep evidence and approval separate

A provider badge or saved draft is not proof that the client outcome is complete. A person still reviews missing evidence and client-facing reports before treating the handoff as ready.

What the buyer gets

The deliverable is a clearer operating workspace and its ownership/review path. Replaced subscriptions, rep hours and revenue gains need their own before-and-after records.

Inside the system

  • Client ownership Links client and staff records to the responsible account owner.
  • Provider receipts Keeps collection results and missing data visible.
  • Versioned reports Gives a reviewer a specific draft and checks delivery prerequisites.
  • Scoped knowledge Separates private ingestion from reviewed shared context.
